Project Overview

A practical guide for busy teachers

Why I started this project
Teachers want multilingual learners to participate fully in classroom learning, but adapting materials takes time, training, and energy. A lesson may ask students to understand new vocabulary, follow several directions, discuss an idea, and explain their thinking in writing—all while learning new content. For my Capstone Project, I am creating a practical guide to help teachers use AI as a planning tool for these language demands. Teachers can begin with materials they already use and draft vocabulary supports, sentence frames, discussion prompts, family communication, or student-friendly directions while keeping the original learning goal.

What this guide helps teachers do
The Teacher Tool helps teachers identify a language need and write a focused prompt with their lesson context, students, and desired support in mind. AI can provide a first draft; teachers then check content accuracy, maintain grade-level expectations, and revise the language for their own students. This includes drawing on students’ home languages, experiences, and strengths. The separate Energy Example shows this process through my PLTW Energy Transfer in Collisions materials and a proposed investigation talk mat. My goal is to make the starting point more manageable while keeping teacher judgment and student learning at the center.

Making planning more manageable

Heavy workloads can affect teacher well-being (Creagh et al., 2025). Research suggests that AI, paired with guidance, can reduce time spent preparing lessons (Roy et al., 2024). This project explores that potential while keeping teacher judgment and revision central.

Teacher Workflow

From your lesson to a support students can use

Build on lesson materials you already use. Create a focused prompt, then apply your teacher judgment to revise the AI draft for your students.

  1. 01

    Start with your existing materials

    Begin with a lesson plan, activity, reading, or directions you already have. Keep the original learning goal and identify where students need language support to access the content and share their thinking.

  2. 02

    Build a better prompt

    A clearer, more specific prompt helps AI produce more relevant results. Use the Prompt Builder below to organize your lesson materials, learning goal, and students’ language needs. Copy the prompt into an AI tool to request a first draft.

  3. 03

    Make teacher revision essential

    The AI draft is a starting point; your judgment makes it useful for your class. Use the Teacher Revision Checklist to review accuracy, learning goals, language support, and student fit. Decide what to keep, revise, or remove before classroom use.

Teacher Prompt Builder

Add your lesson material and language-learning need to build a prompt you can copy into an AI tool. This builder creates the prompt; you review and revise the AI draft afterward.

Prompt Framework

From a lesson idea to an instructional plan

Choose the language task and amount of support your students need. The builder adds a specific output structure, scaffolding directions, and checks for the AI to follow. CRAFT—Context, Role, Audience, Format, and Tone—provides the foundation. This is a guided prompt tool using preset instructional strategies; it does not call AI or analyze your lesson itself.

Citation: Joshi, Jocius, Albert, Joswick, and Blanton (2026), CRAFT Prompt Generation Framework for Teachers .
