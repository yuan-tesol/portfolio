Energy Transfer in Collisions: Compact Teacher Guide

This example uses the compact teacher guide for Part 1: Design Your Own Investigation. In this section, students investigate what happens when objects collide, then use evidence to explain how energy transfers from one object to another.

I chose this section because it includes a clear inquiry sequence: Ask, Predict, Investigate, Observe, and Explain. The task is hands-on, but the language demand is high. Multilingual learners may understand the investigation through observation and collaboration, while still needing support with vocabulary, prediction language, evidence statements, and explanation.

Focus for modification
  • Testable question: What happens when objects collide?
  • Vocabulary: collision, variable, trend, evidence, energy transfer
  • Student writing: prediction, observation, claim, and explanation
  • Collaboration: sharing results with another group

Revision notes

What I Changed and Why

I used the AI-generated materials as a starting point, then revised them based on the lesson goals and my knowledge of my multilingual learners. I simplified the learning objectives, reduced text-heavy vocabulary explanations, and added visuals, Notice/Wonder, Turn & Talk, and sentence starters. I also combined the separate observation sheet and talk mat because they repeated many of the same steps. The revised tool allows students to sketch, record observations, rehearse orally, and develop a claim with evidence and reasoning in one place. I intentionally reduced the amount of language support while preserving the cognitive demand of the science task. Finally, I added vocabulary review and an exit ticket so I could check whether students met both the content and language objectives.
